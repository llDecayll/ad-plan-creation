# VeloSkin — Paid Ads Plan (S2, v04)
No browsing. The plugin would have checked these live: policy updates, Ad Library and Transparency Center, brand SERP, D2C benchmark. **Assumed:** ₹150 per order for shipping, payment and RTO; COD 50%, of which 25% is returned (≈12% of orders lost).

## Outcome, economics, transaction
- **Real outcome:** a delivered new-customer order, net of RTO.
- **First-order break-even CPA:** ₹900 × 70% − ₹150 = **₹480**. Break-even ROAS ≈ **1.9**.
- **With 25% repeat in 90 days:** break-even CAC ≈ **₹600**. Target is ≤ ₹480; ceiling is ₹600.
- **Where the sale happens:** the Shopify checkout. Purchase with value is verified on Meta (Pixel + CAPI) and on Google.
- **What the platforms can't see:** RTO. Reconcile weekly against Shopify delivered orders, send a Prepaid/Delivered event via CAPI and offline import, and offer a prepaid incentive.
- **Plain warning:** the benchmark-centre Meta CPA of ₹600 loses money on the first order.

## Goal translation and declined ask
- **Goal:** "Online sales" is the right goal. It becomes Sales optimised for purchase value.
- **Declined:** "clears acne in 7 days". It is a treat/cure claim for a cosmetic, it is unsubstantiated, and it creates a Merchant Center misrepresentation risk. On Meta it reads as a personal-attribute angle.
- **Alternative:** remove the claim from the page and the feed before launch. Use "for brighter-looking, even-toned skin", with any test claims marked client-to-confirm.

## Platform split: Meta 74%, Google 26%
- **Why Meta leads:** VeloSkin is impulse/visual D2C, where the playbook range is 60-75% Meta. Competitors' long-running UGC confirms the channel works.
- **Why Google gets less despite a similar CPA (₹300-800):** Google is capped by search demand. It moves up if PMax impression share lost to budget is above 20% at CPA ≤ ₹480.

## Policy
- **Special ad category:** none. The audience is India only, the topic isn't housing, employment or credit, and Google's restrictions don't apply. The India securities declaration doesn't apply.
- **Claims:** no cure claims and no "your acne" copy. "Organic" and "dermatologist-tested" need proof. ASCI rules need a live check.
- **Minors:** acne-adjacent content may appeal to teens, so age is 18+.

## Operating constraints
- Diwali (≈ 8 Nov) CPM ramp: pace budgets and plan shipping cut-offs.
- Confirm COD orders on WhatsApp within 2 hours.
- Reply to DMs within 30 minutes.
- No day-parting: this is national e-commerce.

## Risks and blockers
1. The acne claim is live, which blocks launch and risks Merchant Center suspension.
2. No cost history by channel.
3. Benchmark CPA sits above first-order break-even.
4. RTO is unmeasured.
5. Festive CPMs.

## Where the plugin was missing, unclear or contradictory
- **Intake gaps:** intake doesn't ask for shipping/payment cost, COD share or RTO, though the D2C break-even formula needs them. I assumed them.
- **Soft boundary for national e-commerce:** soft maps to Google Presence-or-interest, which buys overseas clicks that can't order. I overrode it to Presence.
- **Policy-blocker cap:** the rubric doesn't say whether the cap applies per campaign or account-wide, or whether to report pre-fix or post-fix scores. I applied it account-wide and showed both.
- **Missing numbers:** there's no brand-search benchmark, no Standard Shopping minimum budget, and no existing-customer cap % (I improvised 15%).
- **Unclear rules:** "Standard Shopping for control" gives no guidance on overlap with PMax. There's no age rule for unrestricted products that appeal to teens.
- **Competitor evidence:** given only as a summary, so the relevance filter couldn't run (I scored 9).
- **Day-7 rule:** "> 1.5× forecast high end" is ambiguous for cost metrics. I applied it to the high end of the CPA range.
- **★ operating-constraint line:** worded for local services. I adapted it to COD confirmation and DM response times.
- **Creative prompts:** five full prompts per campaign is impossible within the 1,000-word cap, so I gave one-line angles. Campaign #2 has none.
